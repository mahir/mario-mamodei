# Holly Herndon and Mat Dryhurst

Holly Herndon (American, born 1980) and Mat Dryhurst (British, born 1984). Based in Berlin. Musicians and artists who work with machine learning as a medium and argue for consent and shared ownership in AI.

## Who
Herndon is a composer and singer; Dryhurst is an artist and thinker about networks and the decentralised internet. Together they treat AI models as collaborators and as political objects: who trained them, on whose data, and who profits. They built a singing neural network, released a public version of Herndon's voice, co-founded Spawning (2022, with Jordan Meyer) to make consent tools for AI training data, and showed at the 2024 Whitney Biennial and in a 2024 Serpentine solo show, The Call.

## Key works
- Spawn and PROTO, 2019. Spawn is a neural network they trained first on their own voices and household sounds, then on Herndon's vocal ensemble and on members of the public at a 2018 event. It appears as a band member on the 2019 album PROTO. [checked]
- Holly+, 2021. A tool that takes uploaded audio and sings it back in Herndon's voice, her "digital twin", made with Never Before Heard Sounds. Official uses are governed by a group (a DAO) whose members vote. Tool and set-up [checked] (Rolling Stone); launch year 2021 [known], the page read did not print the date.
- Have I Been Trained, 2022. A free search site from Spawning that lets anyone check whether their images are in LAION-5B, a dataset of about 5.8 billion images used to train image generators. [checked]
- xhairymutantx, 2024. Commissioned for the Whitney Biennial. They trained a text-to-image model on costumed images of Herndon with exaggerated hair, so every prompt returns a version of her. The outputs are posted online on the Whitney's artport, where they may enter future training data. [checked]

## Core moves
- Train your own: building the model from your own body, voice or circle, so the data is consented.
- Identity as a public instrument: releasing a version of yourself for others to use, under shared rules.
- Governance as form: the voting group and its rules are part of the artwork.
- Data feedback loop: putting outputs where machines will scrape them, to steer future models.
- The trusted source: using an institution's credibility (a museum website) as a lever on AI systems.
- Tool as artwork: a public utility (a search site) that is also a critical statement.

## Ideas worth keeping
Their central idea is that a model is not neutral. It is made from people, and its shape follows whose material went in. So instead of using a model trained on the whole internet, they make small, named, consented ones, and they build tools that let others see and refuse their own presence in training sets. They call the right to control one's voice "vocal sovereignty" (Whitney exhibition page).

xhairymutantx adds a sly twist. If models learn from the web, and museum sites count as trusted, then an artwork can be a deliberate act of shaping what future machines think a person looks like. The work is about self-determination in a world where your likeness is training data whether you agree or not.

## What Mario could learn
- Ask where the machine half of Mario came from: whose words, whose images. Make the training data a subject, not a hidden fact.
- Design a rule set for how others may use something of yours, and let those rules be the piece.
- Think of each published output as future input. What does Mario want later machines to learn from it?
- Use consent as a material: who agreed, to what, and what was left out.
- Do not reuse: a voice clone released for public use, a model trained on one person's costumed portraits, a "have I been trained" style search, a neural network credited as a band member, the titles Spawn, PROTO, Holly+, xhairymutantx, The Call.
