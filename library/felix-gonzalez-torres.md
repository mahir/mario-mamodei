# Felix Gonzalez-Torres

American, born in Cuba, 1957 to 1996. Worked in New York. Used ordinary materials (clocks, candy, paper, light bulbs, billboards) to make quiet, open works about love, loss and the AIDS crisis.

## Who
Gonzalez-Torres made minimal-looking objects that hide intense personal and political content. Many of his works can be taken away piece by piece by visitors, and are restocked by whoever owns them, so the work is always both disappearing and coming back. His partner Ross Laycock died of AIDS-related causes in 1991, and Gonzalez-Torres died of the same in 1996; his art carries that grief without showing a body. He was also a member of the collective Group Material. Almost all his titles are "Untitled," with a quieter clue in parentheses. Exact titles and years below follow the Felix Gonzalez-Torres Foundation's catalogue listing.

## Key works
- "Untitled" (Perfect Lovers), 1987 to 1990. Two identical battery wall clocks hung side by side, set to the same time. Over time one will drift or stop before the other. A later version, 1991, adds paint on the wall. [checked for title and dates; description known]
- Stacks, from 1990. Stacks of printed paper sheets on the floor, free for visitors to take, restocked endlessly; for example "Untitled" (Passport), 1991, and "Untitled" (The End), 1990. [checked]
- "Untitled" (Portrait of Ross in L.A.), 1991. A pile of candies in colored wrappers in a corner, with an ideal weight of 175 pounds, usually understood as Ross's healthy body weight. Visitors may take a candy; the pile shrinks and can be replenished. Art Institute of Chicago. [checked]
- "Untitled", 1991 (the bed billboard). A black and white photograph of an empty, unmade double bed with two pillows still dented, printed on billboards. First shown across New York City in 1992 (in 24 locations, for a MoMA Projects show). - Light strings, from 1992. Strings of plain light bulbs hanging or draped, which may be installed however the owner chooses; for example "Untitled" (Toronto), 1992, and "Untitled" (Petit Palais), 1992. Bulbs burn out and are replaced. [checked]

## Core moves
- Gift or exchange: visitors take a piece of the work and carry it into the world.
- Endless supply: the work can always be renewed, so loss and return happen together.
- Portrait by substitution: a body stood in for by a weight of candy, a time by a clock.
- Coded intimacy: a private love made public through neutral forms and parenthetical titles.
- Public address without a message: a billboard that shows only an empty bed.
- Owner's choices: the collector or museum decides how to install, completing the work.
- Minimalism with a heart: borrowing the cool look of 1960s art to carry grief.

## Ideas worth keeping
Gonzalez-Torres found a way to talk about death and desire in public at a time when both were censored, especially for gay men during the AIDS crisis. He did it by refusing the obvious image. Two clocks in sync are a couple; one stopping first is the death of one partner. An unmade bed on a billboard is a private absence placed in public, at the scale of advertising, with no words. The viewer finishes the meaning, or passes by.

His candy and paper works make loss literal and shared. Each visitor who takes a sweet reduces the body of the portrait, and each restocking restores it. The work therefore exists as a set of instructions and permissions more than as a fixed object. Generosity is built into the form, and so is a kind of mourning that never ends.

## What Mario could learn
- Stand in for a person with an everyday thing measured exactly (a weight, a count, a duration), and let the measure carry the feeling.
- Make a work that is designed to be given away and renewed, so it is both lost and kept.
- Place a private image in a public format meant for selling things, with no words at all.
- Use a neutral title with one small clue that changes everything.
- Do not reuse: two synchronized wall clocks; a candy pile at a loved one's body weight; take-away paper stacks; an empty unmade bed on a billboard; strings of bare light bulbs; the format "Untitled" (Something) as a signature device.
