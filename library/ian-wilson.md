# Ian Wilson

British, born in South Africa, 1940 to 2020. Born in Durban, moved to the United States in 1960, lived in New York from 1966 to 1986. Made spoken conversation his only medium.

## Who
Wilson trained as a painter and gave up making objects in 1968. From then on his art was oral communication: first a single spoken word, then formal group conversations he called Discussions. They were never recorded or transcribed; only invitation cards and signed certificates remain. He is the purest case of Conceptual art with no physical residue.

## Key works
- Circle on the Floor and Circle on the Wall, 1968. Chalk circles in unlimited editions, installed by instruction. His last physical works. [checked]
- Time, 1968. He slipped the word "time" into informal conversations at openings, in the street and in homes. The work was the spoken word and the exchange it set off. [checked]
- Discussion with Michel Claura, January 1970. Held for the Paris exhibition 18 Paris IV.70. The record, typed and signed, notes that the collector Giuseppe Panza bought the discussion in 1977; the topic is unknown. [checked]
- Discussion: April 8, 1973. A discussion with Panza, part of the Known and Unknown Discussions; only a typed certificate survives. [checked]
- Time (spoken), dated 1983. A different, later work: an instruction carried out by gallery staff, who answer visitors' questions about Wilson's contribution by saying it is the word "time" spoken. Shown in Paris in 1983 and revived later. [checked]
- The Pure Awareness of the Absolute / Discussions, 2011 to 2013. A series of invitation-only, 45-minute Discussions at Dia:Beacon. [checked]

## Core moves
- Oral communication: speech as the whole material.
- No residue: refusing recording, transcript or image.
- Certificate as trace: a typed record that a conversation happened, with no content.
- Sale of the immaterial: a collector buys a past conversation.
- Instruction to staff: the institution itself speaks the work.
- Withdrawal: leaving objects for something that exists only while it happens.

## Ideas worth keeping
Wilson asks whether a work can exist only in the minds of people present, and then vanish. A Discussion has a date, participants and a certificate, but its content is lost on purpose. What survives is proof that something meaningful happened and a total blank where the meaning was. The certificate becomes a kind of tombstone for a conversation.

His early Time piece shows how small the unit of art can be: one word, dropped into ordinary talk, noticed or not. He said: "I like the word when it is spoken: 'time'" (Jan Mot). Later, from 1999, the Discussions turned to "the Absolute", the known and the unknown, which makes their content as unreachable as their record.

## What Mario could learn
- Make a work that happens only once between people and leaves a record of the fact, not the content.
- Hand the speaking of a work to someone else (staff, a stranger) and let it pass through them.
- Treat a document that proves an event while hiding its substance as a real artwork.
- Think about what is sold when nothing is delivered: time, presence, trust.
- Do not reuse: the spoken word "time" as an artwork; unrecorded Discussions about the Absolute; certificates that a named collector "purchased the discussion"; gallery staff telling visitors that the work is a spoken word; chalk circles on floor or wall.
