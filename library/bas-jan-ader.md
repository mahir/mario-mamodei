# Bas Jan Ader

Dutch, born 19 April 1942 in Winschoten, the Netherlands; lost at sea in 1975, aged 33. Worked in Los Angeles and the Netherlands. Conceptual artist of falling, failure and feeling.

## Who
Ader studied in Los Angeles (Otis, then Claremont) and made short films and photographs, mostly of himself, in which he falls, gives in to gravity or simply cries. The works are deadpan and spare, close in look to other conceptual art of the time, yet they are about emotion, risk and loss, which most of his peers avoided. In 1975 he set out to cross the Atlantic alone in a tiny boat as part of a work, and was never seen again. That ending colors everything he made.

## Key works
- Fall 1, Los Angeles, 1970. A short film: Ader sits on a chair on the sloped roof of his house, slides, and falls off into the bushes below. [checked]
- Fall 2, Amsterdam, 1970. A short film: Ader rides a bicycle straight into an Amsterdam canal. [checked]
- I'm too sad to tell you, 1970 to 1971. A three-minute silent black and white film of Ader's face, close up, as he weeps; also a photograph and a postcard with the title phrase written on it, sent to friends. [checked for 1971 film and study print; the postcard's 1970 date known]
- Broken Fall (organic), 1971. Ader hangs from a tree branch over a creek until his grip fails and he drops into the water. [checked]
- Broken Fall (geometric), Westkapelle, Holland, 1971. Ader stands by a striped roadside post near the coast, wavers, and falls sideways onto it. [known]
- Thoughts Unsaid, Then Forgotten, 1973. A lamp on a tripod lights the title painted on a wall, beside flowers in a vase that wilt as the show goes on. [checked]
- In Search of the Miraculous, 1973 to 1975. Planned as a triptych. Part one (1973), One Night in Los Angeles, is a series of night photos of Ader walking through the city with a flashlight, captioned with handwritten lyrics from the Coasters' song "Searchin'." Part two was a solo Atlantic crossing: he left Cape Cod on 9 July 1975 in Ocean Wave, a 13-foot sailboat. The boat was found on 18 April 1976 off the coast of Ireland. A planned final part in Groningen never took place. [checked]

## Core moves
- Gravity as subject: let the body give way, and film it plainly.
- Failure on purpose: a staged loss of control that is still real.
- Deadpan sincerity: strong feeling shown with no explanation, in the cool style of conceptual art.
- Withholding: the title says he is too sad to tell you, and he does not tell you.
- Endurance until it breaks: hold on until you cannot.
- Life as the final medium: a work whose last part could not be documented.
- Slow decay: an object (flowers) losing form over the run of a show.

## Ideas worth keeping
Ader found the edge where a simple physical event becomes an image of the human condition. A man falls off a roof: it is slapstick, a test of gravity, and a picture of surrender all at once. He hangs from a branch, and the work is the moment when will gives out. The films are short because the event is short. Nothing is added.

His crying film is a key piece of conceptual art about emotion. It refuses the cause. We see pure sadness with no story, and the title turns the refusal into the work. His last piece pushed this to the end. A search for "the miraculous" that ends in disappearance leaves the question of whether the work failed or was completed. The absence of a record became the record.

## What Mario could learn
- Choose one bodily event (falling, letting go, waiting) and let it stand for something larger without saying what.
- Show a feeling while withholding its reason, and make the withholding the title.
- Build a work in parts, where the last part cannot be documented or might not happen.
- Use a gradual physical change (wilting, melting, fading) to carry an unspoken thought.
- Do not reuse: falling from a roof on a chair; cycling into a canal; hanging from a branch over water; a close-up film of weeping; "I'm too sad to tell you"; night walks with a flashlight and song lyrics; a lone sea crossing as an artwork; the title In Search of the Miraculous.
