# Xu Bing

Chinese, born 1955 (the Met gives Sichuan Province; Art21 gives Chongqing, then part of Sichuan), grew up in Beijing. Trained as a printmaker at the Central Academy of Fine Arts. Lived in New York for nearly two decades after 1989, now based in Beijing.

## Who
Xu Bing makes art about writing: how it carries authority, how it shapes identity, and what happens when it stops making sense. His invented characters and hybrid scripts came out of China's 1980s art boom and the long official campaigns to reform the Chinese script. After the 1989 Tiananmen crackdown made life harder for experimental artists, he moved to New York, where his work turned to the meeting and clash of Chinese and English. He matters for the conceptual tradition because he made language itself the material, using traditional craft rather than plain typescript.

## Key works
- Book from the Sky, about 1987 to 1991, first shown in Beijing in 1988. Hand-printed books and long scrolls in the style of classical Chinese editions, using about 4,000 characters he invented and carved as movable type. Every character looks real and none can be read. [checked]
- A Case Study of Transference, 1994, Beijing. A performance, now a ten-minute video, in which two pigs, one stamped with nonsense Roman letters and one with nonsense Chinese characters, mate in a pen in front of an audience. The Guggenheim New York withdrew the video from Art and China after 1989 (2017) after threats, then acquired it in 2018. [checked]
- Square Word Calligraphy, from 1994. English words written as if they were Chinese characters, so a Western reader can learn to "read" them with a little effort. [checked]
- Background Story, from 2004. Shadows cast on a screen look like a classical Song dynasty landscape painting, while behind the screen the image turns out to be made of twigs, old newspaper and plastic wrap. [checked]

## Core moves
- Illegible authority: perfect form with no meaning, to show how much respect form commands.
- Language as object: script treated as image, material and craft.
- Hybrid system: fuse two writing systems so neither side can read it fully alone.
- Labor as argument: years of skilled carving spent on nonsense.
- Front and back: a beautiful surface that reveals humble rubbish when you walk behind it.
- Transfer: culture shown as something passed on bodily, even crudely.

## Ideas worth keeping
Book from the Sky asks why we trust writing. A Chinese reader who walks into the room sees what looks like an ancient learned text, with all the signs of scholarship, yet cannot read a single word. The experience is both humbling and freeing: the authority of text turns out to sit in its look and its layout as much as in its content. Coming after decades of political slogans and script reform, the work quietly empties the power of words.

His later hybrids reverse the problem. Square Word Calligraphy lets an English speaker feel what it is to struggle into a foreign script, and Background Story shows that a revered tradition can be rebuilt from waste and light. Art21 sums up the throughline: "playing with systems of language and disrupting viewers' expectations" (Art21 artist page).

## What Mario could learn
- Build something with all the outward signs of authority but no content, and watch what respect remains.
- Combine two systems (alphabets, units, codes) so each audience is half inside and half outside.
- Let a viewer discover the cheap or hidden underside of a refined surface.
- Spend obvious care on something "useless" and let the effort carry the meaning.
- Do not reuse: books or scrolls of invented unreadable characters, English letters arranged as Chinese characters, stamped pigs mating, a lightbox landscape made of debris seen from behind.
