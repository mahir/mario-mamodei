# Collective Actions and Andrei Monastyrski

Russian group founded in Moscow in March 1976, led by Andrei Monastyrski (born 1949 as Andrei Sumnin). Central to Moscow Conceptualism; still active.

## Who
Collective Actions (Kollektivnye Deistviia, KD) was founded by Andrei Monastyrski, Nikita Alekseev, Georgy Kizevalter and Lev Rubinstein (who took part only in the first action). Nikolai Panitkov joined in 1977; Elena Elagina, Igor Makarevich and Sergei Romashko in 1979; Sabine Hänsgen in 1985. Under Soviet rule they could not show in public, so they invited small groups of friends by word of mouth to fields and woods outside Moscow. There, very little happened. Monastyrski is the group's leader and only permanent member, as well as a poet and writer.

## Key works
- Monastyrski, Pile (Kucha), 1975, part of his Elementary Poetry series (1975 to 1983). Visitors added small objects (a match, a button, a coin) to a pile; each gift was logged and receipted. [checked]
- Appearance, 13 March 1976, a snowy field in Izmailovo Park, Moscow. About thirty invited people stood and looked at the empty field. After a while two figures appeared far away, walked toward them and handed out signed certificates confirming they had attended. [checked]
- Lieblich, 2 April 1976. An electric bell buried under snow in a field rang without stop; guests heard it but could not see it. [checked]
- Slogan-1977, 1977. A text banner hung among trees in the woods, followed by later slogan actions (Slogan, 9 April 1978; Four Slogans, 2018). [checked]
- Trips Out of Town, from 1980. The samizdat volumes that collect each action's description, place, date, participants, photos, and participants' own accounts. The documentation is as much the work as the event. [checked]
- Monastyrski, Kashirskoe Highway (Kashira Highway), written 1983 to 1986. A book recording his mental crisis of 1982 as a diary. [checked]

## Core moves
- Empty action: an event with almost no content, so attention turns to waiting, seeing and expecting.
- Absence: the important thing is out of sight (a buried bell, distant figures).
- Certification: a receipt or certificate that proves you were there.
- The rule and its record: every action is followed by careful written accounts from those who attended.
- Small trusted audience: art for friends, outside the reach of the state.
- Landscape as stage: a blank field of snow as the work's empty page.

## Ideas worth keeping
Collective Actions found freedom in emptiness. In a society full of official meaning, a snowy field where two people slowly walk toward you is close to a pure experience of time and expectation. The group called these "empty actions": the meaning is not given but produced in the viewer's mind, then written down. Monastyrski called the idea "just an excuse in order to generate this feeling of eventfulness" (quoted in ArtFocusNow).

The documentation is the other half. Participants wrote their memories of each action, and the group gathered them into volumes. So the work lives in many slightly different accounts, a kind of shared, uncertain memory. This anticipates later debates about whether the event or its record is the art.

## What Mario could learn
- Design an event in which almost nothing happens, and make the waiting the subject.
- Hide the key element (a sound, a person, an object) so it exists only through its effects.
- Give the audience a document that certifies they witnessed something nearly invisible.
- Let several imperfect testimonies stand in for the event instead of one image.
- Do not reuse: two figures crossing a snowy field to hand out certificates, a bell buried in snow, banners in the forest, receipted piles of small donated objects, the title Trips Out of Town.
