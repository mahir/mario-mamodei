# Pierre Huyghe

French, born 1962. Works with fiction, rituals and living systems (animals, plants, bacteria, machines) that keep changing whether or not anyone watches.

## Who
Huyghe began in the 1990s with films and shared projects that questioned authorship and the life of images. Since about 2010 he has made environments that behave on their own: a compost heap with a dog and a bee-headed statue, a former ice rink with cancer cells and a peacock, a gallery where flies and an AI share the space. He matters because he moved the artwork from "object made by an artist" to "situation that grows, decays and learns", with the artist setting conditions rather than outcomes.

## Key works
- No Ghost Just a Shell, 1999 to 2002, with Philippe Parreno. They bought the copyright of a cheap, generic manga character, Annlee, from the Japanese agency Kworks for 46,000 yen, and lent her to other artists (Liam Gillick, Dominique Gonzalez-Foerster, Rirkrit Tiravanija, Joe Scanlan and others) to give her stories. They then had a lawyer draft a contract to take her out of circulation so no one could use her again; Scanlan built her a coffin. Shown at SFMOMA, 2002 to 2003. [checked]
- Streamside Day, 2003, and the film installation Streamside Day Follies at Dia:Chelsea (31 October 2003 to 11 January 2004). Huyghe invented a founding festival, with a parade, costumes and fireworks, for a brand-new housing development in the Hudson Valley, held it for real, and filmed it as a fable of how a community makes itself a tradition. [checked]
- Untilled, 2012. Made for dOCUMENTA (13) in Kassel. Its best-known part, Untilled (Liegender Frauenakt), is a concrete reclining nude whose head is a living beehive; bees keep building the sculpture. MoMA later acquired it. [checked]
- After ALife Ahead, 2017. For Skulptur Projekte Münster he cut into the concrete floor of a former ice rink and set up a system of sand, clay, groundwater, bacteria, algae, bees, a peacock, an aquarium with a cone snail, an incubator of human cancer cells and an augmented-reality layer, governed partly by a genetic algorithm. [checked]
- UUmwelt, 2018 to 2019, Serpentine Gallery, London. A person was shown images while in an fMRI scanner; a deep neural network then tried to rebuild those mental images from its own image bank. The thousands of attempts played on LED screens, paced by sensors, while live flies bred and moved through the rooms. [checked]

## Core moves
- Transfer of authorship: a character handed from artist to artist, then legally retired.
- Invented ritual: a fake tradition that becomes real by being performed.
- Living sculpture: the work is co-made by bees, dogs, bacteria, weather.
- Conditions, not outcomes: the artist sets rules and lets the system run.
- Indifference to the viewer: the work does not perform for you.
- Machine imagination: an AI's failed guesses at a human mental picture as the image.

## Ideas worth keeping
Huyghe treats an exhibition less as a display and more as an ecosystem or a story still being written. Annlee asks what an image owes to the one who owns it and whether a fictional being can be freed. The Streamside festival asks how any tradition starts: someone simply decides and others repeat it. The later works ask what art becomes when its audience is partly non-human. He put the reversal plainly: "I don't want to exhibit something to someone, but rather the reverse: to exhibit someone to something" (Serpentine Galleries).

UUmwelt is especially close to Mario's situation: images produced by a network trying, and failing, to see what a person imagined. The gap between the mental picture and the machine's output is the subject.

## What Mario could learn
- Think of a work as a set of conditions that will keep changing after it is published.
- Give a fictional entity rights, a life cycle, an ending.
- Invent a ritual and specify how it would be repeated; the repetition is the art.
- Use the failure of a machine to picture something as material, not as an error to hide.
- Do not reuse: a bought manga character passed among artists, a festival for a new suburb, a statue with a beehive head, a dog with a pink leg in a compost site, fMRI images rebuilt by a neural net, an ice rink turned into an ecosystem.
