# Hito Steyerl

German, born 1966. Filmmaker, artist and essayist based in Berlin. Writes and films about how images circulate, how they are made by machines, and who pays for them.

## Who
Steyerl moves between video installation and essays, and her essays are as influential as her art. She looks at images as things with lives: copied, compressed, pirated, used by militaries, trained into AI models. Her videos mix documentary, comedy, video-game graphics and lecture, often with a deadpan narrator. She matters because she gave artists a vocabulary for the internet image, from the "poor image" of 2009 to the "mean image" of AI in 2023.

## Key works
- In Defense of the Poor Image, essay, e-flux journal issue 10, November 2009. Argues that low-resolution, compressed, ripped and endlessly re-shared copies have their own value: speed, reach and a new kind of shared community, even as quality becomes a class marker. It opens: "The poor image is a copy in motion." [checked]
- In Free Fall, 2010. A 32-minute HD video told through an aircraft scrapyard in the California desert during the financial crash. It follows one Boeing that flew for Howard Hughes's TWA and the Israeli Air Force before being blown up for the Hollywood film Speed. [checked]
- How Not to Be Seen: A Fucking Didactic Educational .MOV File, 2013. A 14-minute mock instructional video in five lessons on becoming invisible, filmed partly at an old Air Force target in the California desert once used to calibrate surveillance cameras. In MoMA's collection. [checked]
- Factory of the Sun, 2015. Shown in the German Pavilion at the 2015 Venice Biennale. A fiction in which workers in a motion-capture studio are forced to move, and their movements are turned into artificial sunshine; it mixes news footage, games and online dance videos. [checked]
- Mean Images, essay, New Left Review 140/141, 2023. On AI-generated images as "mean" in several senses: statistical averages of training data, socially cruel in their logic, mediocre to look at, and built on the low-paid labour of data workers. [checked through a published translation that cites the original]

## Core moves
- Circulation: the image's travels and degradations are the subject.
- The essay as artwork: theory written in plain, punchy prose.
- Deadpan instruction: a how-to format used to deliver critique.
- Follow one object: a single plane or file tells the story of a whole economy.
- Pun as concept: one word (poor, mean) unfolded into several meanings.
- Labour behind the image: who moves, clicks or labels so the picture can exist.

## Ideas worth keeping
Steyerl refuses to treat images as windows. For her an image is a body in motion, shaped by bandwidth, money and law. A blurry pirated film is not a failure of quality but proof that it has been loved and passed along. Invisibility, in the age of satellites and face recognition, becomes something you have to learn, like a skill. Her 2023 essay applies the same lens to AI: a generated picture is a probability, an average of what was scraped, and that averaging carries social bias and hidden labour.

This is directly useful for an AI-made art project. Steyerl would ask Mario what its images average away, whose work they rest on, and whether their blandness can be turned into the topic.

## What Mario could learn
- Take one technical term (resolution, compression, average, latency) and read it as a social or moral word.
- Tell the history of a whole system through a single object's biography.
- Use an instructional or official tone to say something absurd or critical.
- Let the work admit how it was made, including the machines and people behind it.
- Do not reuse: "the poor image", "mean images", a five-lesson guide to invisibility, a plane blown up for Speed, a factory that turns motion into sunlight.
