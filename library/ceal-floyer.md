# Ceal Floyer

British, born 1968 in Karachi, Pakistan; died December 2025. Lived and worked in Berlin. Made very spare works in which an ordinary object, sound or word is shifted just enough to become a joke, a puzzle and a thought.

## Who
Floyer worked with the smallest possible means: a slide projector, a CD player, a receipt, a box of pens. Each work takes something literally, or sets an object to describe itself, so the viewer is caught between what they see and what they know. She emerged in London in the 1990s and showed with Lisson Gallery from 1997 onward. She matters because she pushed the deadpan, language-based side of Conceptual art into a form that is funny, precise and almost silent.

## Key works
- Light Switch, 1992 to 1999 (Tate's dating). A slide projection of a light switch, thrown onto the wall at the height where a real switch would be. The image is made of light, so the "switch" exists only while the projector is on. [checked for title and date; description known]
- Light, 1994. A single bulb, not connected to power, lit from four sides by slide projectors. [checked]
- Unfinished, 1995. A close-up looped video of someone twiddling their thumbs; shown at the 1995 Venice Biennale. In SFMOMA's collection. [checked]
- Monochrome Till Receipt (White), 1999. A supermarket receipt listing only white items, shown on the wall like a small monochrome painting. [checked]
- Bucket, 1999. A black bucket with a CD player inside, playing the sound of a single drop of water falling every few seconds, as if from a leak above. [checked]
- 'Til I Get It Right, 2005. A sound piece looping a line from a Tammy Wynette country song, with a phrase edited out, so the song keeps circling without arriving. [checked]
- Ink on Paper, 2010. Felt-tip pens stood upright on blotting paper until their ink drained into stains, the colours set by the pens in the box. [checked]
- Overgrown and Things are also cited as her works, but I could not confirm their years or what they look like. [verify]

## Core moves
- Literalism: take a word or phrase at its exact meaning (ink on paper, light switch).
- Representation in place of the thing: the projected image of an object where the object should be.
- Sound standing in for an event: a leak heard, never seen.
- Category as rule: a shopping list chosen by colour becomes a monochrome.
- The endless loop: a sentence edited so it never quite arrives.
- Minimal means: one projector, one receipt, one bucket.

## Ideas worth keeping
Floyer's works sit in the gap between a thing and its description. The receipt is a real record of a real purchase and also a white painting, made by a cashier's printer. The projected switch has the same shape as a real one but turns nothing on: it is pure picture, powered by the very electricity it seems to control. The bucket makes you look up for a leak that is only in the speaker. Her jokes are never only jokes; they show how quickly we fill in what we expect.

She summed up the stance in one line: "There's a fine line between making sense of the world and making nonsense of it" (Lisson Gallery).

## What Mario could learn
- Choose a common phrase and make the image that takes it exactly at its word.
- Let a document (a receipt, a label, a ticket) become a picture by choosing what is listed on it.
- Replace an event with its sound or trace, and let the viewer supply the rest.
- Loop or cut a familiar sentence so it can never resolve.
- Do not reuse: a projected light switch, a till receipt of white goods, a bucket playing a drip, pens bleeding on blotting paper, a looped Tammy Wynette line, a lit unplugged bulb.
