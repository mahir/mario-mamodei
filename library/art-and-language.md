# Art & Language

British collective, founded 1968 in Coventry, England, with a New York branch in the 1970s. The most theory-driven group of Conceptual art.

## Who
Art & Language was founded in Coventry in 1968 by Terry Atkinson, David Bainbridge, Michael Baldwin and Harold Hurrell, who taught and studied around Coventry College of Art. Its journal Art-Language, first issued in May 1969, treated writing and argument as the art itself. Up to fifty people used the name between 1968 and 1982; since 1977 the group has mainly been Michael Baldwin and Mel Ramsden, with the critic Charles Harrison (died 2009). Later they returned to painting as a way to keep arguing.

## Key works
- Air Conditioning Show / Air Show / Frameworks, 1966 to 1967. Texts by Atkinson and Baldwin proposing an exhibition of air in a conditioned room, and the questions such a show raises. Published in October 1967 in an edition of 200. [checked]
- Map to Not Indicate, 1967. A map by Atkinson and Baldwin that lists many places it does not show. Tate's glossary gives the title as "Map Not to Indicate". [checked]
- Secret Painting, 1967 to 1968, by Mel Ramsden. A black painting paired with a text saying its content is invisible and known only to the artist. Ramsden later joined the group. [checked]
- Art-Language, volume 1, number 1, May 1969. "The journal of conceptual art", published in Coventry, edited by Atkinson, Bainbridge, Baldwin and Hurrell. The issue printed Sol LeWitt's Sentences on Conceptual Art, plus texts by Dan Graham and Lawrence Weiner. [checked]
- Index 01, 1972. Shown at Documenta 5 in Kassel: filing cabinets holding the group's texts, with printed indexes on the wall mapping which texts agreed or clashed with others. [known]
- Index: Incident in a Museum XIX, 1987. One of a series of paintings showing museum rooms with further paintings inside them. Tate collection. [checked]
- Hostage L, 1990. Oil on canvas on wood with glass, from the Hostage series. [checked]

## Core moves
- Conversation as work: the group's talk and writing are the product.
- Index: cross-referencing texts to map agreement and conflict.
- Hidden content: a painting whose subject is withheld by statement.
- Negative mapping: listing what a map leaves out.
- The journal as exhibition space: printed argument replaces the gallery.
- Collective authorship: one name for many changing hands.
- Return to painting with a twist: pictures that comment on their own setting.

## Ideas worth keeping
Art & Language treated art as a question about knowledge: what do we know when we look at a work, and who gets to say? Their early texts read like philosophy papers on purpose. Their key insight is that an art world runs on talk, and that the talk itself can be shown, sorted and indexed. Index 01 made the group's disagreements visible as a structure you could walk around.

Ramsden's Secret Painting turns the private claim of the artist into a public puzzle. You see a black field and a statement that the real content is invisible. It mocks the idea of hidden depth while also making you want it.

## What Mario could learn
- Index a body of statements by how they agree or conflict, and show the index as the work.
- Make a work whose main content is withheld, and make the withholding precise and checkable.
- Map what is left out: list absences instead of presences.
- Let a group voice speak in place of a single author, and show where it disagrees with itself.
- Do not reuse: a black painting whose content is "invisible" and known only to the artist; an exhibition of conditioned air; a map that names what it does not show; filing cabinets of cross-indexed texts; paintings of museum rooms inside paintings; the subtitle "the journal of conceptual art".
