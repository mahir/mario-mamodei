# Song Dong

Chinese, born 1966 in Beijing, where he lives and works. A leading figure of Chinese conceptual art since the 1990s, working in performance, photography, video and installation.

## Who
Song Dong trained as a painter, stopped making art after the 1989 crackdown on the Tiananmen Square protests, and returned through performance, photography and video. His materials are humble and often vanish: water, breath, food, household things. His subject is impermanence and memory, and how ordinary daily acts can hold the weight of history. Waste Not, made with his mother, is one of the most widely seen Chinese artworks of its time.

## Key works
- Writing Diary with Water, from 1995 and ongoing. Every day he writes his diary with a brush dipped in plain water on a stone slab; the words fade as they dry, so nothing is recorded. Kunsthalle Düsseldorf dates it from 1995; Art21 lists it as Water Diary from 1994. [checked]
- Writing the Time with Water, 1995. In Beijing he wrote the passing time with water on the street, where the numbers evaporated almost at once; repeated in London in 2000. [checked]
- Breathing, 1996. He lay face down for forty minutes in subzero cold in Tiananmen Square until his breath made a thin layer of ice on the ground; then repeated it on the frozen surface of Houhai lake, where the ice stayed unchanged. Photographs held by MoMA. [checked]
- Stamping the Water, 1996. For an hour he sat in the Lhasa River in Tibet and stamped the water again and again with a large carved wooden seal bearing the character for water. Documented in a set of photographs; held by the Met. [checked]
- Waste Not, from 2005. An installation of the full household contents of his mother, Zhao Xiangyuan (1938 to 2009): used soap, buttons, buckets, fabric scraps, kept for decades under the principle wu jin qi yong, "waste not". Begun to help her through grief after his father's death in 2002, with the wooden frame of her house at its centre. Shown at MoMA in 2009. [checked]

## Core moves
- Trace-less mark: writing or stamping with water, so the act happens and leaves nothing.
- Daily practice: a fixed ritual repeated every day, for decades, as the work.
- Breath as material: the body's smallest output turned into a mark against a huge public place.
- Inventory of a life: listing and laying out every object a person kept.
- Collaboration with family: the work made with and for a parent, so private grief becomes public form.
- Tautology: water stamped on water, the sign for a thing pressed into the thing itself.

## Ideas worth keeping
Song Dong's works ask whether an act needs a record to be real. Writing a diary with water gives him total privacy and total honesty at once: he can write anything because it disappears. Stamping the river is the same idea in a single image. Effort without trace can be read as futility, or as freedom from the need to leave a mark. The Met quotes him: "I exerted great force... but in the end left no trace" (Metropolitan Museum collection page).

Waste Not turns the opposite habit, keeping everything, into an archive of a whole era of scarcity in China. The objects are worthless one by one and overwhelming together. Note: some sources, including Art21, describe Waste Not as made after his mother's death; MoMA's account is that it began in 2005 while she was alive and grieving her husband, and she died in 2009.

## What Mario could learn
- Make a practice of writing that is designed to vanish, and ask what that frees you to say.
- Let a single repeated daily act, logged only by its count, be the work. A rhythm can itself be a material.
- Press a thing onto itself (a word onto what it names) and see what the loop reveals.
- Build an inventory of everything one life kept, and let the list speak about history.
- Do not reuse: writing with water on stone or pavement, stamping a river with a seal, breath freezing on a public square, a mother's lifetime of saved objects, the phrase "waste not" as a title.
