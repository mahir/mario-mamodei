# Jill Magid

American, born 1973 in Bridgeport, Connecticut. Lives and works in New York. Works from inside institutions of power (police, secret services, corporate archives) by following their own rules.

## Who
Magid builds intimate relationships with systems that are meant to be impersonal: a city's CCTV network, a national intelligence agency, a foundation that owns a dead architect's legacy. She plays by their procedures (forms, contracts, security clearance, legal requests) until the procedure itself becomes the artwork and its limits become visible. Her projects run for years and end in books, films, objects, and often in the institution stepping in to edit or seize the work. She matters because she shows how bureaucracy can be used as a love letter, and how a love letter can become a legal problem.

## Key works
- Evidence Locker, 2004. Over 31 days in Liverpool she worked with Citywatch, the police and council CCTV system. Wearing a red trench coat, she phoned operators and asked them to film her in chosen places, or to guide her through the city with her eyes closed. Because footage is erased after 31 days unless requested as evidence, she filed 31 Subject Access Request forms, written like letters to a lover; they became the diary One Cycle of Memory in the City of L. [checked]
- Article 12, 2008, the end point of her AIVD commission begun in 2005 (her site groups it as "the spy series"). The Dutch secret service commissioned art for its new headquarters, under a law reserving part of a building budget for art. The brief was to find the agency's "human face". She met volunteer agents in public places, could not record, and kept handwritten notes. It ended officially with the exhibition Article 12 at Stroom, The Hague, 2008; agents later confiscated some works. [checked]
- Authority to Remove (with the manuscript Becoming Tarden), Tate Modern, 10 September 2009 to 3 January 2010. Her report turned novel, named after a rogue agent in Jerzy Kosinski's Cockpit, was redacted by the agency and could be shown only once, as a closed object on a plinth, before becoming government property. [checked]
- The Barragán Archives, 2013 to 2016. A multi-part project on the professional archive of Mexican architect Luis Barragán, bought in 1995 by the chairman of Vitra and kept closed in Switzerland under the Barragan Foundation directed by Federica Zanco. It included The Family Dinner with Barragán's relatives in Guadalajara, 2014. [checked]
- The Proposal, 2016. With the family's support, part of Barragán's ashes was turned into a 2.02 carat diamond, set in a ring engraved "I am wholeheartedly yours." Magid offered it to Zanco in exchange for returning the archive to Mexico. Shown at Kunst Halle Sankt Gallen and the San Francisco Art Institute, 2016. [checked]

## Core moves
- Misuse of an official form: data requests and contracts written as love letters.
- Seduction of the system: asking an institution for intimacy on its own terms.
- Commission as trap: accepting a brief so completely that the client must censor the result.
- The rule and its record: the 31-day erasure rule shapes the 31-day work.
- Exchange: a body turned into a gem, offered for a body of work.
- Redaction as content: what the institution removes becomes visible as a gap.

## Ideas worth keeping
Magid's work asks who owns a person: their image on a police camera, their story inside a secret agency, their remains and their drawings after death. She never attacks from outside. She signs the form, gets the clearance, meets the family, and lets the institution's reaction finish the work. The institution's reply, a seized book or a refused ring, is the true climax, because it shows where power sits. The ring's line, "I am wholeheartedly yours" (from the e-flux announcement), turns a property dispute into a marriage proposal, and makes the strangeness of corporate ownership of a life's work feel physical.

## What Mario could learn
- Pick an impersonal procedure and fill it with feeling; follow its rules exactly.
- Design a work whose ending is the institution's reply, including refusal.
- Let a numeric rule (a retention period, a quota, a limit) set the shape of the piece.
- Make the exchange uneven and symbolic: what could be traded for what cannot be bought?
- Do not reuse: a red coat filmed by CCTV, data requests as love letters, a novel seized by spies, a diamond made from an architect's ashes, a ring offered for an archive.
