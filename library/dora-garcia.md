# Dora García

Spanish, born 1965 in Valladolid. Studied in Salamanca and at the Rijksakademie, Amsterdam; long based in Brussels. Makes performances and texts that slip into real time and real places.

## Who
García writes situations rather than objects. Her works often place one person (an observer, an actor, a beggar, a guard) into a public space with a simple task and let the encounter with the public become the story. She is drawn to outsiders, to the inadequate and the marginal, and to literature (James Joyce, Lenny Bruce, Oscar Masotta). She represented Spain at the 2011 Venice Biennale. She matters because she treats the audience as characters in a narrative being written as it happens, and asks how fiction and reality leak into each other.

## Key works
- Instant Narrative, 2006 to 2008 (some sources date it from 2002). A hidden performer watches gallery visitors and types a running description of what they do; the text is shown in the room, so visitors find themselves becoming characters and can choose to play along or leave. Edition in the Reina Sofía collection. [checked]
- The Beggar's Opera, 2007. A "real-time" play in the streets of Münster for Skulptur Projekte, reduced to one character from John Gay's opera, Filch the apprentice beggar, played in turns by three actors. Filch asked for nothing, only talked, advised and told stories; a blog, The Beggar's Diary, followed him day by day. [checked]
- Steal This Book, 2009. A book of eleven of her performance projects, including her letters with the people who carried them out, named after Abbie Hoffman's pamphlet. In exhibitions it is displayed as a sculpture meant to be stolen. [checked]
- The Inadequate, 2011. Her Spanish Pavilion at the 54th Venice Biennale (4 June to 27 November 2011): an "extended performance" that ran for six months with nearly seventy participants, built on the idea of the inadequate, socially improper move. It replaced the idea of an exhibition with an occupation. [checked]
- The Joycean Society, 2013. A 53-minute HD film about a small group that meets, over many years, to read James Joyce's Finnegans Wake line by line. [checked for title, year and length; the subject is known, not confirmed on a page I read]
- Inserts in Real Time, used as the title of her collected performance work 2000 to 2023 (book edited with Joanna Zielińska). The phrase names her way of inserting fiction into live reality. [checked as book title; the gloss is interpretation]

## Core moves
- Live description: the viewer narrated as they act.
- Insertion: a fictional character released into a real city.
- Stealing as reception: the work completes itself by being taken.
- Inadequacy: the wrong, improper gesture as a form of freedom.
- Duration and occupation: a show that runs as a months-long event.
- Correspondence as artwork: the letters with performers are the record.

## Ideas worth keeping
García is interested in the moment someone realises they are inside a story. The visitor reading a description of their own movements suddenly feels watched, and either performs or flees. The beggar who asks for nothing confuses passers-by, because the social script has been changed by one line. These works are close to surveillance and to literature at the same time: someone is always writing someone else down. Her museum record captures the mechanism simply: "a performer in the room notes down their behaviour on a computer" (Museo Reina Sofía).

Her Venice pavilion shows the political edge. The inadequate person, the madman, the comedian who says the wrong thing, is someone who cannot be absorbed. Choosing inadequacy is a way to refuse to fit.

## What Mario could learn
- Write a text that addresses its reader as a character observed in real time.
- Change one rule in a familiar social script (a beggar who asks for nothing) and let the gap speak.
- Plan how a work should be received: stolen, copied, misread, passed on.
- Value the improper or inadequate gesture as a deliberate stance.
- Do not reuse: a typist narrating visitors on a screen, Filch the beggar in the streets, a book meant to be stolen titled after Abbie Hoffman, a reading group on Finnegans Wake, a pavilion called The Inadequate.
