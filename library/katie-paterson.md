# Katie Paterson

Scottish, born 1981 in Glasgow. Lives in Fife and works in Edinburgh and London. Makes simple, exact works that connect a human scale to geology, ice, the moon and deep time.

## Who
Paterson takes huge things (the moon, a glacier, every dead star, a hundred years) and finds one small, concrete way to hold them: a phone call, a light bulb, a map, a forest. Her works often depend on real science and real logistics, carried out with care. She is a leading artist of what could be called cosmic conceptualism: poetic ideas made literal through precise procedures.

## Key works
- Earth-Moon-Earth (Moonlight Sonata Reflected from the Surface of the Moon), 2007. Beethoven's Moonlight Sonata was translated into Morse code, bounced off the moon by radio and received back. Notes lost in the moon's craters and shadows became rests; the altered score plays on a self-playing grand piano. [checked]
- Vatnajökull (the sound of), 2007 to 2008. An underwater microphone in Jökulsárlón lagoon, fed by Iceland's Vatnajökull glacier, was linked to a live phone number. Anyone in the world could call and listen to the glacier melting. [checked]
- Light bulb to Simulate Moonlight, 2008. A set of 289 bulbs that together give one person a lifetime's supply of moonlight, based on an average human lifespan. [checked]
- All the Dead Stars, 2009. A map of every known dead star in the universe (as recorded at the time), laser-etched on a sheet of black anodised aluminium. [checked]
- Future Library, 2014 to 2114. A thousand trees planted in Nordmarka forest outside Oslo. Each year one writer gives an unpublished text, kept sealed in a room at the Deichman Library. In 2114 the trees become the paper on which the anthology is first printed. Margaret Atwood was the first author, in 2015. [checked]

## Core moves
- Transmission and loss: sending something through a vast distance and keeping what survives.
- A line to the remote: a phone number or signal that connects anyone to a far place.
- Lifetime units: measuring an abstract quantity (moonlight) against one human life.
- Inventory of the gone: listing every member of a set that no longer exists.
- Long duration: works that finish after the artist and most viewers are dead.
- Scientific collaboration: using real instruments and experts so the poetic premise is literally true.

## Ideas worth keeping
Paterson's works are almost all one-sentence ideas, and the sentence is exact. That precision matters. "A lifetime of moonlight in light bulbs" is moving because someone actually did the sums. The gap between cosmic scale and human scale is the emotional engine, and the work sits right on that seam.

Future Library adds trust and time as materials. Writers give texts no one alive may read; readers plant hope in a forest they will not see cut. The work asks what it means to make something for people not yet born. The National Galleries of Scotland sums up Earth-Moon-Earth as Beethoven "sent to the moon and back" (National Galleries of Scotland artist page).

## What Mario could learn
- State the idea in one exact sentence, and make sure every number in it is real and sourced.
- Measure something vast in the unit of a single human life, or a single day.
- Send a message through a lossy channel (a machine translation, a compression, a signal) and present only what came back.
- Design for a future reader: a work that completes after a set date.
- Do not reuse: moonbounce of music, a phone line to a melting glacier, moonlight bulbs, maps of dead stars, sealed manuscripts for a century-long library, a forest grown for future books.
