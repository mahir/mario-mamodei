# Francis Alÿs

Belgian, born 1959 in Antwerp. Trained as an architect; moved to Mexico City in the mid 1980s and has made most of his work there. Known for walks and simple actions that become fables.

## Who
Alÿs makes art out of walking and doing small, often pointless things in public, then lets the story of the action spread like a rumour. A film, a postcard, a painting or a retelling is what remains. His actions are tiny compared with the problems they touch: urban poverty, borders, failed modernisation, war. He matters because he found a poetic way to make political art without slogans, and because he understood that an action's afterlife as a story can be bigger than the action itself.

## Key works
- The Collector, 1991 (often dated 1990 to 1992). Made with Felipe Sanabria in Mexico City. Alÿs walked the streets pulling a small magnetic "dog" on wheels that picked up metal scraps until it was fully covered. [checked]
- Paradox of Praxis 1 (Sometimes Making Something Leads to Nothing), 1997. He pushed a large block of ice through the streets of Mexico City for hours until it melted into a puddle. [checked]
- Re-enactments, 2000. A two-channel video (some sources date it 2001). He bought a gun and walked through Mexico City with it until police seized him after about eleven minutes; shortly after, the police helped him restage the arrest for the camera, this time with a replica. [checked]
- When Faith Moves Mountains, 2002. In Lima, about 500 student volunteers with shovels worked their way across a large sand dune in a line, shifting it a few centimetres. Alÿs called it "land art for the landless". [checked]
- The Green Line, 2004. In June 2004 he walked the part of the 1948 armistice line that runs through Jerusalem carrying a leaking can of green paint, using 58 litres to trace 24 km. The film was later shown to invited respondents, from historians to a Knesset member, whose reactions became part of the work. [checked]

## Core moves
- The walk as drawing: a line traced by moving through a city.
- Futile labour: great effort for almost no result.
- Rumour or hearsay: the story of the act travels further than the act.
- Reenactment: the same event staged twice, the second time as fiction with the real actors.
- Collective gesture: many people doing one small task together.
- Poetic politics: the slogan replaced by a parable.

## Ideas worth keeping
Alÿs is the great artist of disproportion. A melting block of ice stands for years of work that leave nothing; a line of shovels barely moves a dune but moves the people holding them. The gap between effort and result is the meaning. He is honest about this: the poetic act does not fix anything, but it lets a situation be seen and retold.

Re-enactments raises a sharp question about documentation. If the police will cheerfully restage an arrest for the camera, what do any news images prove? The Green Line asks what a line on a map does to people on the ground, simply by drawing it again in paint. The title of the project's book says it best: "Sometimes doing something poetic can become political" (francisalys.com).

## What Mario could learn
- Find an action whose effort is huge and whose result is almost nothing; write it as a parable.
- Let a line on a map become a real path, and ask who it divides.
- Design the story first: how would this act be retold by someone who only heard about it?
- Use repetition to test truth: stage the same event twice and compare.
- Do not reuse: pushing ice until it melts, a magnetic dog collecting scraps, 500 people moving a dune, a dripping paint can along the Green Line, walking with a loaded gun until arrested, the phrase "land art for the landless".
