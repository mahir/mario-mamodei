# Cory Arcangel

American, born 1978 in Buffalo, New York. Long based in Brooklyn, now in Stavanger, Norway. A programmer-artist who hacks games, software and online culture.

## Who
Arcangel came out of the early 2000s scene of artists who opened up old game cartridges and rewrote them. He works like a hobbyist programmer and a deadpan comedian at once, using default tools and obsolete hardware to make work about attention, boredom and how fast technology ages. His 2011 Whitney show, Pro Tools, made him a central figure for art about consumer software. His pieces are often simple to describe and hard to forget.

## Key works
- Super Mario Clouds, 2002. A hacked Super Mario Bros. cartridge with everything removed except the scrolling clouds and blue sky, shown on a Nintendo NES. In the Whitney collection. [checked]
- Totally Fucked, 2003. Another modified Mario cartridge, in which Mario is stuck on a cube with nowhere to go. [checked]
- Photoshop Gradient Demonstrations, from 2008. Large unique prints of colour fades made with Photoshop's standard gradient tool; each title records the settings used, for example Photoshop CS: 84 by 66 inches... (2010). [checked]
- Drei Klavierstücke op. 11, 2009. Schoenberg's 1909 atonal piano pieces rebuilt note by note from YouTube clips of cats walking on pianos. [checked]
- Working on my Novel, 2009. A collection of Twitter posts by people saying they are working on their novel. [checked]
- Various Self Playing Bowling Games, 2011. Projected bowling video games from the late 1970s to the 2000s, each hacked to throw only gutter balls, shown in historical order. [checked]

## Core moves
- Subtraction hack: removing everything from a program except one element, so the leftover becomes the work.
- The default tool: using a software preset with no personal touch, and recording the settings as the title.
- Found crowd: collecting many strangers' posts or clips and arranging them into a single form.
- Rebuilt score: reconstructing a serious work of culture from trivial online material.
- Programmed failure: making a machine fail forever (gutter balls) on purpose.
- Obsolete hardware: using dead consoles and formats to show how fast "new" becomes old.

## Ideas worth keeping
Arcangel treats software as a readymade. Photoshop's gradient tool, a Nintendo cartridge or YouTube's cat videos are already culture, made by thousands of people. By choosing one and changing a single thing, he shows how much of our creativity is shaped by the defaults of tools. The gradient prints ask whether art made by clicking a preset is less personal than a brushstroke, and the titles answer by listing the click.

There is also tenderness in the work. Super Mario Clouds keeps only the part of the game nobody looked at, the background, and makes it the whole view. The cat Schoenberg is both a joke and a sincere act of devotion that took months. He has said: "Music is about attention. So you have to be aware of it" (Lisson Gallery artist page).

## What Mario could learn
- Find the ignored background of a familiar system and make it the subject.
- Let a title be the exact settings or parameters that produced the image. A recipe is a portrait of the tool.
- Rebuild something grand out of tiny, silly, collective fragments, and take the task seriously.
- Program a loop of guaranteed failure and let it run as a quiet tragedy.
- Do not reuse: Mario clouds or any stripped Mario game, Photoshop gradient prints with settings as titles, cats playing Schoenberg, gutter-ball bowling games, collected "working on my novel" posts.
