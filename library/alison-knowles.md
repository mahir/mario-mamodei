# Alison Knowles

American, 1933 to 2025. Founding member of Fluxus in New York, known for event scores about food, daily routine and chance, and for an early computer poem.

## Who
Knowles took part in the first Fluxus performances in 1962 and stayed with the movement for six decades. She made art out of the most ordinary things: a salad, a lunch order, beans, paper. Her scores are generous and social; many end in food that the audience shares. With the composer James Tenney she also made one of the first poems generated by a computer. She died in New York on 29 October 2025, aged 92.

## Key works
- Proposition (#2, Make a Salad), 1962. A one-line score: make a salad. First performed at the Festival of Misfits, Institute of Contemporary Arts, London, on 24 October 1962. Later versions fed hundreds of people with salad mixed in huge quantities. [checked]
- Bean Rolls, 1963. A Fluxus multiple: dried beans in a reused tin, with small paper rolls carrying her research on beans across cultures. A version is dated 1965 as part of a Fluxkit. [checked]
- The Identical Lunch, from 1967. Knowles noticed she ordered the same lunch at a diner near her Chelsea home almost every day. In 1968 Philip Corner pointed out that the habit was already an event score. She wrote it up as a score, invited others to eat the same lunch and record it, and compiled a Journal of the Identical Lunch in 1971. [checked]
- The House of Dust, 1967. A computer poem made with James Tenney, written in Fortran and run on a Siemens 4004. Each stanza begins "a house of" and draws at random from lists of materials, places, light sources and inhabitants. In 1968, with a Guggenheim Fellowship, she built a physical house in Chelsea based on it; after arson damage it was moved to CalArts, where she taught in the early 1970s. [checked]
- The Big Book, 1967. A walk-in book, large enough to move through, with pages as rooms. [checked for title and year only]

## Core moves
- Instruction: a score short enough to remember and do at once.
- Routine as found art: a habit becomes a work once it is named and repeated on purpose.
- Gift or exchange: the performance ends in shared food.
- Combinatory system: a small vocabulary plus a rule generates endless variations.
- Record of repetition: a journal tracks the same act done many times by different people.
- From text to space: a poem becomes a building, a book becomes a room.

## Ideas worth keeping
Knowles shows that an artwork can be something you already do. The Identical Lunch was not invented; it was noticed, and a friend's remark turned a habit into a score. Repetition, once made deliberate, makes small differences visible: the same sandwich is never the same twice, because the day, the bread and the eater change.

The House of Dust is the same idea at machine speed. A few short lists, combined by chance, produce a flood of houses no one designed. Then she did something unusual: she built one. The leap from a generated line of text to a real structure that people can sit in, damaged by fire, moved, repaired, gives the poem a life story. A rule-based text does not have to stay on paper.

## What Mario could learn
- Look for a habit that is already a work, and name it so others can repeat and record it.
- Build a tiny generator: a fixed opening plus three or four lists, and let one output become physical.
- End a work in something shared and used up, so the piece disappears into its audience.
- Track one repeated act across many people and let the variations be the content.
- Do not reuse: making a salad as a score; the same tuna fish sandwich ordered daily; the opening "a house of" with material, place, light and inhabitants; a walk-through giant book; beans in a tin.
