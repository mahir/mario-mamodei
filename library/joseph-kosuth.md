# Joseph Kosuth

American, born 1945. Based in New York and Europe. Leading theorist of Conceptual art as an inquiry into language and definition.

## Who
Kosuth argued that art after Duchamp is about the concept of art itself, and that its proper tools are those of language and philosophy. While still in his early twenties he made works from dictionary definitions, neon words and photostats, and wrote the essay "Art after Philosophy". He was one of the four artists in Seth Siegelaub's January 5 to 31, 1969 exhibition. His work keeps asking where meaning sits: in the object, the picture, or the words.

## Key works
- One and Three Chairs, 1965. A wooden folding chair, a photograph of a chair and a photographic enlargement of the dictionary entry for "chair", shown side by side. Which of the three is the chair? [checked]
- Five Words in Green Neon, 1965. A neon sign that spells out its own title, so the description and the thing described are the same. [checked]
- Titled (Art as Idea as Idea) [meaning], 1968. A large photostat of a dictionary definition, mounted on board. One of a long series in which a definition replaces the picture. [checked]
- Art after Philosophy, 1969. An essay published in Studio International, beginning in the October 1969 issue (volume 178, number 915, page 134), and continued in the following issues. It argues that art works as a kind of definition. [checked]
- January 5 to 31, 1969. Siegelaub's exhibition in a rented space at 44 East 52nd Street, New York, with Robert Barry, Douglas Huebler and Lawrence Weiner, where the catalogue was the main event. [checked]
- The Second Investigation, 1968 to 1969. Categories from a thesaurus placed as paid advertisements in newspapers and other public media, so the work appears outside the gallery in borrowed space. [known]

## Core moves
- Definition as work: a dictionary entry stands in for the object or the image.
- Tautology: a work that says exactly what it is.
- Substitution: object, photograph and text compete to be the "real" thing.
- Borrowed media: art placed in ad space, where it looks like information.
- Theory as practice: the essay is part of the art, not a comment on it.
- Interchangeable parts: the chair and photo can be swapped; the idea stays.

## Ideas worth keeping
Kosuth's best works are traps for the eye that end in a question about words. Faced with a chair, its picture and its definition, you start ranking them and realise that each is a different kind of claim. None of them holds the whole chair. The work becomes a small lesson in how language, images and things each hand you only part of the world.

He also pushed the view that art is a set of propositions about art. The Whitney quotes his later summary of Conceptual art as "the understanding of the linguistic nature of all art propositions". The risk of this view is dryness; the reward is a clear test for any work: what is it claiming, and how does it make that claim?

## What Mario could learn
- Put two or three forms of the same thing side by side and let the gaps between them do the work.
- Ask what a work claims, then find the plainest form that makes that claim.
- Place a work where information usually appears (classifieds, forms, reference books) and let the setting change how it reads.
- Make a work that describes itself, then look for the place where the description fails.
- Do not reuse: an object plus its photograph plus its dictionary definition; neon that spells its own title or word count; enlarged dictionary definitions on photostat; the title "Art as Idea as Idea"; thesaurus categories bought as newspaper ads; the phrase "art after philosophy".
