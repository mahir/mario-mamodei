# Robert Barry

American, born 1936. Based in New York and New Jersey; taught at Hunter College from 1964 to 1979. Pioneer of invisible and immaterial Conceptual art.

## Who
Barry began as a Minimalist painter and, around 1968, turned to materials that cannot be seen: radio waves, radiation, inert gases, thought. By 1969 he removed material reference altogether and made works of language. He was one of the four artists in Seth Siegelaub's January 5 to 31, 1969 exhibition. His works ask how much of an artwork can disappear before it stops existing.

## Key works
- 88 mc Carrier Wave (FM) and 1600 kc Carrier Wave (AM), 1968. Radio carrier waves as sculpture: present in the room but impossible to see. [checked]
- One Million Dots, 1968. His contribution to Siegelaub's Xerox Book. [checked]
- Inert Gas Series/Helium, Neon, Argon, Krypton, Xenon/From a Measured Volume to Indefinite Expansion, 1969. Measured amounts of five noble gases released into the air around Los Angeles. A poster gave only a post office box and a phone number that played a recorded description. [checked]
- All the things I know but of which I am not at the moment thinking, 1:36 PM; June 15, 1969. A pencilled wall text naming an unseen, shifting body of knowledge. [checked]
- Telepathic Piece, 1969. A work that existed through a catalogue entry: Barry would try to communicate an artwork by telepathy. [known]
- Closed Gallery Piece, 1969 to 1970. Invitation cards saying the gallery would be closed during the exhibition, for shows in Amsterdam (Art & Project, bulletin 17, December 1969), Turin (Sperone) and Los Angeles (Eugenia Butler Gallery, 1970). [checked]
- Art Work, 1970. A short text describing an artwork that is always changing and has no fixed boundaries; best known from the catalogue of MoMA's Information show, 1970. [checked]

## Core moves
- Invisibility: real but unseen materials (gas, waves, energy).
- Absence: the closed gallery as the exhibition.
- Release: a measured amount set free to spread without limit.
- Thought as medium: telepathy, knowledge not currently in mind.
- Minimal announcement: a poster, a card, a phone message as the visible trace.
- Indeterminacy: a work defined by what it cannot fix or show.

## Ideas worth keeping
Barry tests the edge of existence for an artwork. A gas released into the sky is still there, in some sense, everywhere and nowhere. A closed gallery is still an exhibition, if an announcement says so. A list of everything he knows but is not thinking about is real and enormous, yet cannot be shown. Each work depends on trust in a statement, and on the viewer's willingness to imagine what is missing.

The visible parts of his works are often almost administrative: a card, a poster, a recorded message. These small traces are what make the invisible part social. The plainest case is the card that simply read: "During the exhibition the gallery will be closed" (as described by visualartsdb).

## What Mario could learn
- Choose a material that is real but cannot be seen, and design only its announcement.
- Let the smallest possible trace (a card, a voicemail, a date and time) carry a vast or invisible work.
- Make a work out of what is not present in mind or place at a given moment.
- Use release instead of display: set something loose and let it spread beyond control.
- Do not reuse: a gallery closed for the show; releasing noble gases into the sky; carrier wave pieces; telepathic transmission of a work; "all the things I know but of which I am not at the moment thinking"; a phone number that plays the work's description.
