# Ryan Gander

British, born 1976. Makes works built from loose associations, invented artists, near-invisible gestures and stories the viewer must finish.

## Who
Gander is a storyteller who uses exhibitions as plots. He invents artists and movements, hides works behind curtains and frosted glass, and makes rooms that seem empty. His method, which he first set out in slide lectures, is to jump from one cultural reference to another until a private logic appears. He matters because he shows how much of an artwork happens in the viewer's head: the guess, the rumour, the missing piece.

## Key works
- Loose Associations Lecture, 2002, collected in Loose Associations and other lectures (Onestar Press, Paris, 2007). A slide talk that links unrelated things (Tolkien, the TV detective Inspector Morse, London's Barbican Centre, his Aunty Deva) into one chain of thought. It opens, roughly, by admitting that the links "may be a bit loose" (quoted in frieze). [checked]
- The Best Club, 2011. A blackout curtain with a plaque describing a film by an invented Austrian artist; pull back the curtain and there is only a wall. [checked]
- Locked Room Scenario, 2011. An Artangel commission in a Hoxton warehouse, London (29 August to 23 October 2011). Visitors booked to see a group show by an invented movement, the Blue Conceptualists, but found it apparently closed: works glimpsed through blinds and frosted glass, unopened letters, a ringing phone behind a locked door, people who might or might not be actors. [checked]
- I Need Some Meaning I Can Memorise (The Invisible Pull), 2012. At dOCUMENTA (13), the ground floor of the Fridericianum in Kassel was left almost empty except for a steady, engineered breeze that drew visitors through the rooms. It took years of engineering to make. [checked]
- Associative Ghost Templates #2 to 5, 2012. Layered clear acrylic sheets with numbered openings and text on the back, describing faint objects that are barely there (shown in The Fallout of Living, Lisson Gallery, 2012). Note: the usual title is "Associative Ghost Templates", not "Associative Templates". [checked]
- Kodak Courage, 2012. An oak case with smart glass that fogs over, hiding a small stone carving of the artist's nose made in the manner of Rodin. [checked]

## Core moves
- Loose association: meaning built by jumping between unrelated references.
- Invented artists: fictional colleagues, movements and histories presented as real.
- The withheld work: curtains, fogged glass, locked rooms; you see that something is there but not what.
- Invisible material: a breeze, an absence, a description of an object instead of the object.
- Clues and fragments: the viewer as detective completing the plot.
- The cost of nothing: years of engineering spent on air.

## Ideas worth keeping
Gander's works are like detective novels with the last chapter torn out. He knows that a hidden thing is more vivid than a shown one, because the viewer's imagination fills it. An empty museum floor with a wind blowing through it becomes a work about attention and value: the market wants objects, and he offers air. The invented Blue Conceptualists, with their memoirs and letters, make the point that art history itself is partly a story agreed upon.

His lectures are also a method worth stealing in spirit: put two things side by side that have no right to meet, and talk until the connection feels inevitable.

## What Mario could learn
- Write a text that describes an object the reader will never see; let the description be the work.
- Build an artist's backstory and let traces of it (letters, labels, rumours) imply the rest.
- Chain unrelated references and make the reader feel the hidden logic.
- Treat something invisible (air, pressure, delay) as a physical material.
- Do not reuse: a breeze in an empty Kassel gallery, a locked show by the Blue Conceptualists, a curtain hiding a blank wall, a fogging vitrine over a carved nose, a slide lecture that opens "Hello, I'm Ryan".
