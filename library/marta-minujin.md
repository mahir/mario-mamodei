# Marta Minujín

Argentine, born 1943 in Buenos Aires, where she lives and works. Pioneer of happenings, immersive environments and huge public monuments made to be taken apart.

## Who
Minujín came out of the 1960s Buenos Aires scene around the Instituto Torcuato Di Tella and spent time in Paris and New York. Her work is loud, pop and playful, but it often carries a sharp political point about media, debt or censorship. Many of her largest works are temporary structures the public is invited to climb, enter, eat or dismantle. Tate holds documentation of The Parthenon of Books, and the Jewish Museum in New York recently gave her a first US survey.

## Key works
- La destrucción (The Destruction), 1963. Her first major happening, in Paris: she invited other artists to help set fire to her own sculptures in an empty lot. [checked]
- La Menesunda, 1965, with Rubén Santantonín, at the Di Tella Institute, Buenos Aires. A maze of eleven rooms, each a different sensory situation. The title is Buenos Aires slang for a mess or confusion. Rebuilt at the Museo de Arte Moderno de Buenos Aires in 2015. [checked]
- Simultaneidad en simultaneidad (Simultaneity in Simultaneity), 1966. A technically complex media work made in Buenos Aires. [checked for title and year; details of how it worked not confirmed]
- El Partenón de libros (The Parthenon of Books), 1983, Avenida 9 de Julio, Buenos Aires. Built the year democracy returned, a Parthenon-shaped frame covered with books banned under the junta. At the end it was tipped over by cranes and the public took the books. [checked]
- The Parthenon of Books, 2017, documenta 14, Friedrichsplatz, Kassel. A full-size version built from donated books that are or were banned somewhere, on the square where Nazis burned books in 1933. [checked]
- Payment of Argentina's foreign debt to Andy Warhol with corn, 1985. A staged photo in which she hands Warhol corn cobs as "Latin American gold." [verify: exact title wording and date not confirmed on a page read]

## Core moves
- Happening: a one-time event where the audience is inside the work.
- Destruction as making: the work ends by burning, toppling or being given away.
- Monument as gift: build something grand from objects people can take home.
- Site memory: place the work where a historical wrong happened.
- Sensory maze: guide bodies through a sequence of strange rooms.
- Pop absurdity with a serious core: a joke that also states a debt or a ban.

## Ideas worth keeping
Minujín treats the monument as a temporary body that the public eats. The Parthenon of Books stands for democracy, but it only fulfills that meaning when it is taken apart and the banned books scatter into private homes. The work turns a symbol of permanence into an act of distribution. Smithsonian quotes her: "Democracy without books is not democracy" (Smithsonian, An Artist Is Building a Parthenon of Banned Books).

Her environments ask a related question about the spectator. La Menesunda does not show the viewer an idea. It moves them through confusion, so the meaning is the feeling of being jostled by a city full of media and appetite.

## What Mario could learn
- Imagine a monument whose meaning is completed only by its own dismantling.
- Choose a material that is itself the argument (forbidden, scarce, or edible) and build a familiar form from it.
- Think about which place carries the memory of a wrong, and what object could answer it there.
- Use humor to state an unpayable or absurd debt in plain terms.
- Do not reuse: a Parthenon or temple of banned books, a maze of sensory rooms called a mess, burning one's own sculptures, paying a debt with corn, a celebrity receiving produce as currency.
