# Mladen Stilinović

Yugoslav and Croatian, 1947 (born in Belgrade) to 2016 (died in Pula). Worked in Zagreb as a key figure of the New Art Practice in socialist Yugoslavia.

## Who
Stilinović was a co-founder of the Zagreb Group of Six Authors in the second half of the 1970s, with Vlado Martek, Boris Demur, Željko Jerman, Sven Stilinović and Fedor Vučemilović. He worked with cheap means (texts, artist's books, banners, bread, sugar, money, the color red) to pick apart the language of power, first socialist and then capitalist. His dry humor about work, pain and language made him a reference point for artists in Eastern Europe after 1989. Documenta 12 in 2007 brought his work to a wide audience.

## Key works
- Artist at Work, 1978 (artnet gives 1977). A set of eight photographs of the artist lying in bed, asleep or idle. The title presents doing nothing as the job. [checked]
- Sing! (Pjevaj!), 1980. Held by the Carnegie Museum of Art; title and year seen in artnet's obituary, content not described there. [checked for title and year only]
- Exploitation of the Dead, 1984 to 1990. A long cycle that reuses "dead" visual languages (Suprematism, Socialist Realism, geometric abstraction, the star, the cross, red and black) as if collecting relics, with subseries such as Dead Optimism. [checked]
- An Artist Who Cannot Speak English Is No Artist, 1992. The sentence sewn on a banner, in English. It can be read as a demand, a protest or a shrug about the new global art world after 1989. [checked]
- In Praise of Laziness (often titled The Praise of Laziness), text dated 1993. A short essay claiming Western artists are too busy producing, networking and competing to make art, while artists in the East had time to be lazy. It names Duchamp and Malevich as the great masters of laziness. [checked]

## Core moves
- Idleness as work: present rest and inaction as the artist's real labor.
- Language as object: a single sentence on a banner, treated as a found official slogan.
- Exploiting dead signs: reuse the emptied symbols of ideology and art history as material.
- Poverty of means: cheap, everyday materials as a refusal of the market and of state pomp.
- Deadpan statement: a flat claim that sounds both true and absurd.
- East looks at West: turn the gaze around, so the Western system becomes the strange one.

## Ideas worth keeping
Stilinović understood that every system has a vocabulary and that the vocabulary outlives the belief. Socialist slogans, red stars and abstract squares had once meant revolution; by the 1980s they were shells. Instead of mocking them from outside, he collected and recombined them, showing how meaning drains away while the form keeps working.

After 1989 he applied the same eye to the West. The English banner and the laziness text both name a new rule nobody voted for: that art means constant production in one language. His answer was to praise the one thing the market cannot use, which is time spent doing nothing. The essay states it plainly: "there is no art without laziness" (Stilinović, The Praise of Laziness, via Agosto Foundation).

## What Mario could learn
- Take an exhausted symbol from any ideology and ask what it still does when no one believes it.
- Write one flat sentence that states an unspoken rule of a professional world.
- Treat rest, delay or refusal as a subject that needs images, not as a lack of content.
- Look at a dominant culture from the edge and describe it as if it were the foreign one.
- Do not reuse: an artist photographed asleep as "artist at work," the English-language banner sentence, the praise of laziness essay and its East and West argument, "Exploitation of the Dead," Duchamp and Malevich as lazy masters.
