# John Baldessari

American, 1931 to 2020. Worked in Southern California, taught at CalArts. Brought humour, photography and everyday language into Conceptual art.

## Who
Baldessari was a painter who decided that painting needed a joke and a rule. He hired sign painters to letter texts on canvas, burned his own early paintings, and set himself absurd tasks that a camera recorded. His dry wit made Conceptual art feel like a game you could join. As a teacher in Los Angeles he shaped generations of artists.

## Key works
- What Is Painting, 1966 to 1968. A professional sign painter hand-lettered on canvas a passage from an art appreciation book about how to compose a picture. A painting made of advice about painting. [checked]
- The Cremation Project, 1970. In summer 1970 he had his remaining paintings from May 1953 to March 1966 cremated at a San Diego mortuary, after photographing about 100 of them. Some ashes were baked into cookies; the rest went into signed, numbered boxes, with a plaque giving the dates. Remains were shown in the exhibition Software at the Jewish Museum, New York. [checked]
- I Will Not Make Any More Boring Art, 1971. For the Nova Scotia College of Art and Design, he proposed that students write the sentence over and over on the gallery walls, like a school punishment. He also made a video of himself writing it, and students printed a lithograph from his handwriting. [checked]
- Baldessari Sings LeWitt, 1972. A 12 minute 38 second video in which he sings Sol LeWitt's Sentences on Conceptual Art to well-known tunes. [checked]
- Throwing Three Balls in the Air to Get a Straight Line (Best of Thirty-Six Attempts), 1973. A set of fourteen colour photographs recording an impossible task and its near misses. [checked]

## Core moves
- Delegation: a sign painter, students or a printer makes the work.
- Destruction as new start: ending a body of work as a public act.
- Punishment as form: repetition of a line as penance and pledge.
- The absurd task: a goal that chance will almost never grant.
- Best of: editing many attempts into a selection, and saying so.
- Misuse of an official form: the art textbook, the school exercise, the mortuary.
- Cover version: performing someone else's manifesto in a new voice.

## Ideas worth keeping
Baldessari took serious questions (what is a painting, who makes it, when is a body of work finished) and staged them with props from ordinary life: a sign shop, a classroom, a funeral home. The humour is not a decoration. It lets the audience see the rule clearly, because a rule followed to an absurd end shows its shape.

He also trusted failure. Throwing balls to make a straight line will mostly fail; the title admits that the result is the best of many tries. The record is honest about luck and effort. When he sang LeWitt, he introduced it by saying "these sentences have been hidden too long in exhibition catalogues" (Electronic Arts Intermix). Even homage became a way to test how ideas travel.

## What Mario could learn
- Give a serious question a mundane setting (an office, a shop, a classroom) and let the setting do the arguing.
- Set an almost impossible task, repeat it, and present the best attempt with its count.
- Hand the making to a specific trade or group and make that choice visible.
- End something on purpose and treat the ending as a work with its own record.
- Do not reuse: "I will not make any more boring art" or any line written out as punishment; cremating one's own paintings or baking ashes into food; throwing balls to form a line; a sign painter lettering "what is painting"; singing another artist's manifesto to popular tunes; a dot over a face in a found photo.
