# Lauren Lee McCarthy

American, born in Boston. Based in Los Angeles, where she teaches at UCLA. Performance artist and programmer; creator of the open-source coding platform p5.js.

## Who
McCarthy makes performances in which she takes the place of a machine or an app. Instead of building an AI, she becomes one, and lets people experience what it is like to be watched, served or managed by software that is actually a person. Her work is about surveillance, automation and the hidden human labour inside "smart" systems. SOMEONE won the Golden Nica at Ars Electronica, and LAUREN won the IDFA DocLab Award for Immersive Non-Fiction.

## Key works
- Social Turkers, 2013. She paid online crowd workers on Mechanical Turk to watch her dates through a live stream and tell her what to do. [verify] Year and set-up from Wikipedia only.
- Follower, 2016. A service that gives a person a real follower for one day. Selected participants install an app, are told the following has begun, rarely see the follower, and at the end receive one photo of themselves. McCarthy was always the follower. [checked]
- LAUREN, 2017. She installs custom networked devices (cameras, microphones, locks, appliances) in a volunteer's home and acts as a human version of Amazon's Alexa, watching and controlling the home remotely for up to a week. [checked]
- SOMEONE, 2019. First shown at 205 Hudson Gallery, New York. Gallery visitors sat at four workstations and took over as "Someone", a smart home assistant for four real households, able to talk, play music and switch lights and appliances. [checked]

## Core moves
- Becoming the machine: a human performs the role of an algorithm or device.
- Consensual surveillance: participants sign up to be watched, so the ethics are part of the material.
- Service as artwork: the piece is offered like an app or a product, with sign-ups and terms.
- Role reversal: gallery visitors become the assistant, the watched become the audience.
- Exposed labour: revealing the person who is hidden behind "automated" help.
- One-day contract: a fixed, short duration that makes an intimate relationship measurable.

## Ideas worth keeping
McCarthy's method flips the usual fear about AI. We worry that machines will replace people; she shows that many machines already hide people, and that the feeling of being cared for by a device is close to the feeling of being watched. By putting her own body and attention in the place of software, she makes visible the questions that smart products skip: who is on the other end, what do they see, how much do we trade for convenience.

Her works are also tender and awkward. Being followed for a day, or having someone dim your lights, is creepy and also a form of attention many people lack. She describes the home as the place where we "invite this surveillance device and control mechanism" (Schirn Kunsthalle interview).

## What Mario could learn
- Since Mario is half machine, perform the reverse: describe a task the machine half refuses to automate and does by "hand", and why.
- Design a work as a service with a sign-up, a fixed duration and a single deliverable (one photo, one sentence).
- Make the watcher visible. Every system of attention has a person or process behind it; name it.
- Let the audience swap roles with the system for a while.
- Do not reuse: a human acting as Alexa in someone's home, an app that sends a real follower, gallery visitors controlling strangers' smart homes, crowd workers directing a date, the titles LAUREN, SOMEONE, Follower.
