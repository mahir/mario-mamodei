# Adrian Piper

American, born 1948 in New York. Lives in Berlin. Conceptual artist and analytic philosopher (PhD Harvard 1981) whose work moves from abstract systems to race, identity and the ethics of everyday encounters.

## Who
Piper started in the late 1960s making language and system pieces close to Sol LeWitt. Around 1970 she turned herself into the material, staging strange acts in public to see how people react. Since then her work has dealt with racism and xenophobia, almost always by building a direct encounter between the work and the individual viewer. She is also a Kant scholar. She won the Golden Lion for best artist at the 2015 Venice Biennale, and MoMA gave her a full-floor retrospective, A Synthesis of Intuitions, 1965 to 2016, in 2018.

## Key works
- Catalysis series, 1970 to 1971. Street actions in New York. She rode the subway with clothes soaked in a foul-smelling mix, went shopping in clothes painted white with a "Wet Paint" sign, rode the bus with a towel stuffed in her mouth. Bystanders' reactions were the real subject. [checked for title and dates; details of individual actions known]
- Food for the Spirit, 1971. While fasting, doing yoga and studying Kant's Critique of Pure Reason alone in her loft one summer, she felt her sense of self dissolving and photographed herself in a mirror to confirm she still existed. Fourteen photographs, printed 1997, at MoMA. [checked]
- The Mythic Being, 1973 to 1975. In an Afro wig, mustache and sunglasses, she walked city streets as a young man of color, a persona the public tended to read as a threat, while repeating lines from her own old diary. [checked for title; dates vary by source, 1973 to 1976, verify]
- Funk Lessons, 1982 to 1984 (performance from 1983). Group sessions teaching funk dance and its history to largely white art audiences, turning a party into a lesson about culture and shared joy. [checked]
- My Calling (Card) #1, 1986. A printed business card she hands to a person who has made a racist remark in her company, not knowing she is Black. It states her identity, names the hurt, and politely regrets it. Card #2 (from 1986) answers unwanted advances. [checked]
- The Probable Trust Registry: The Rules of the Game #1 to 3, 2013. Three reception desks, each under a gold wall statement such as a promise always to mean what you say. Visitors can sign a contract with themselves to follow it; the signatures are kept in a registry. Shown at the 2015 Venice Biennale. [checked for title and Golden Lion; 2013 date known]

## Core moves
- Catalysis: a small strange act in public that makes onlookers reveal their assumptions.
- Self as material: the artist's body and self-image as the test site.
- Persona: performing as someone the public will read differently.
- Misuse of an official form: a business card, a contract, a registry used for moral confrontation.
- Encounter: work built for one viewer at a time, who must respond.
- Self-binding: viewers make a promise to themselves; the work stores it.
- Philosophy as structure: Kantian ideas of self and reason shape the setup.

## Ideas worth keeping
Piper's art asks what happens in the space between two people when one has assumed something about the other. Catalysis made strangers the authors of the piece through their reactions. My Calling (Card) took the same idea into dinner parties: it is a polite, standard form that delivers a rebuke, and it moves the discomfort from her to the speaker. The form's calm is part of the point.

The Probable Trust Registry flips the usual relation between artwork and viewer. Instead of judging the art, you decide whether you will bind yourself to a simple rule, knowing your signature will be stored. The work measures not taste but integrity. Across her career the question is the same: what do we owe each other, and how do we show we are the selves we claim to be?

## What Mario could learn
- Design a printed form (a card, a receipt, a pledge) that delivers a moral message in a calm, official tone.
- Make a work completed only by the viewer's choice to commit to something, and imagine what the archive of commitments would show.
- Use a small deviation in public behavior to make onlookers visible to themselves.
- Treat an inner state (losing your sense of self, keeping a promise) as something the work must test or verify.
- Do not reuse: a calling card handed after a racist remark; contracts signed at a reception desk under a gold slogan; a mustache and wig street persona; mirror self-portraits while reading Kant; a "Wet Paint" sign on clothing; funk dance lessons.
