# David Horvitz

American, born 1982 in Los Angeles, where he lives and works. Makes small, often instruction-like works about time, distance, the sea, and how images and ideas spread online.

## Who
Horvitz works in the spirit of 1960s and 1970s Conceptual and Fluxus art (instructions, mail, simple gestures) but uses the channels of the internet age: Wikipedia, stock photography, Flickr tags, email lists. Many works give an idea away for others to carry out. Others play with time zones and the sun, or with seawater as a measure of place. He matters because he shows how a tiny, gentle gesture can travel across a network much further than its author, and become something no one owns.

## Key works
- 241543903, 2009. An idea from his email list For 2009, Idea Subscription: put your head in a freezer, photograph it, and upload the picture with the tag 241543903. Strangers took it up through a Flickr group and a website, and searching the number online still returns heads in freezers. [checked]
- Public Access, 2011 to 2014. Photographs of himself at the shoreline of California's public beaches, back to the camera, uploaded to the Wikipedia articles about those beaches under several accounts. Editors argued over them and removed most; the prints are shown with printouts of the articles. [checked]
- Mood Disorder, uploaded 2012; book 2015 (Chert and Motto Books, Berlin). He studied stock images of depression, then made a deliberately clichéd self-portrait (head in hands, by the sea) and put it on Wikimedia Commons under a free licence. Hundreds of health and lifestyle sites reused it, usually without credit, and he documented the spread. [checked]
- Let us keep our own noon, 2013. A bell was recast into 47 handbells. At local solar noon, 47 performers ring them, then walk apart into the streets, each ringing until they can no longer hear any other bell. The title comes from a 19th-century newspaper headline about a protest against railway standard time. Performed at the New Museum, New York, and at the National Gallery of Iceland. [checked]
- somewhere in between the jurisdiction of time, 2014. Thirty-two glass vessels of Pacific seawater collected along 127.5 degrees west, set in a north to south line at Blum and Poe, Los Angeles, with letters asking to move the area's time zone. [checked]
- Nostalgia, a long-running text project; a version titled Nostalgia (10,080) is dated 2025. [verify: I could not confirm what the work does or when it began]

## Core moves
- Instruction as gift: an idea released for anyone to perform.
- Circulation: a picture or tag spreading across networks as the actual work.
- Misuse of an open platform: Wikipedia and free licences as a distribution system.
- Local time: the sun's noon set against clock time and time zones.
- Measuring with the sea: water as a unit of place and distance.
- Listening until silence: a performance that ends when you can hear only yourself.

## Ideas worth keeping
Horvitz is interested in what happens after an artwork leaves the artist. A head in a freezer becomes a meme with no author attached. A sad stock-style photo ends up illustrating articles about debt and burnout, proving how interchangeable images of feeling are. He named the cliché plainly: "This is a fake sadness" (Aperture). The time works do the same with clocks: they remind you that "noon" was once a local fact, before railways and networks made everyone agree on one time.

His work is gentle, but it tests real systems: what Wikipedia will accept, how far a free image will travel, whether a government will move a time zone for a line of bottles.

## What Mario could learn
- Write an instruction that strangers could perform, and imagine its spread as part of the work.
- Use a cliché image on purpose and follow where it ends up.
- Set personal or solar time against official time.
- Make a performance that ends at a perceptual limit (out of earshot, out of sight).
- Do not reuse: heads in freezers with a number tag, a stock-photo depression self-portrait on Wikipedia, 47 handbells at local noon, bottles of seawater along a longitude, beach selfies on Wikipedia articles.
