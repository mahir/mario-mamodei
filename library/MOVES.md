# Moves

A catalogue of the basic moves conceptual artists make. A move is not a style or a subject. It is the way a work gets its meaning: what it swaps, withholds, counts, hands over or makes impossible. Most strong works use one move cleanly, sometimes two.

Each entry gives the move, who has used it (see their files in this folder), what it opens up, and the trap to avoid. Use it to find an angle, then find a subject the angle has not been used on. Never pair a move with the same subject a listed artist already used.

## Language and naming

**Language as object.** The words are the work. They can be painted, spoken, printed or carried, and need not be built. Weiner, Kosuth, Holzer, Stilinovic, Camnitzer, Xu Bing, Broodthaers. Opens: a sentence can hold a whole sculpture, and the reader builds it. Trap: a clever sentence that is only a slogan.

**Definition and tautology.** A thing presented next to its own definition or description, or a work that says only what it is. Kosuth, Bochner, Song Dong, Creed. Opens: the gap between a thing, its picture and its name. Trap: a dictionary joke with nothing under it.

**The label that denies or misnames.** A caption, title or sign that contradicts or redirects what we see. Duchamp, Broodthaers, Camnitzer, Floyer. Opens: how much a label decides what we see. Trap: surrealism for its own sake.

**Illegible authority.** Text that has every look of meaning and none of the content. Xu Bing, Darboven. Opens: we trust the form of writing before we read it. Trap: decoration.

## Instruction and authorship

**Instruction.** The work is a set of steps someone else may carry out, or may only imagine. LeWitt, Ono, Brecht, Knowles, Shiomi. Opens: the idea is the work; making it is optional. Trap: a to-do list. Mario's texts are never instructions; an instruction can appear inside a work, but the text thinks about it.

**Transfer of authorship.** Someone else makes, finishes, speaks or decides. LeWitt's drafters, Baldessari's sign painters, Gonzalez-Torres's owners, Sehgal's interpreters, Cohen's AARON, Herndon and Dryhurst's Holly+. Opens: who is the artist when the hand is someone else's, or a machine's. Trap: a gimmick about outsourcing.

**Fictional author or institution.** The work is made by an invented person or body. Kabakov's characters, Broodthaers's museum, Ono's imagined performers, and, for that matter, Mario himself. Opens: a fiction can tell the truth better than a report. Trap: lore that replaces the idea.

**Reenactment and cover version.** Remaking or performing someone else's work so the repeat says something new. Baldessari Sings LeWitt, Monk, Arcangel, Alys. Opens: what survives a copy. Trap: homage with no turn. Mario must not remake existing works; use the move only on non-art material (a speech, a recipe, a weather report).

## Absence and the near-invisible

**Absence.** The work is what is missing: an empty room, a closed gallery, a gas released into the air. Barry, Ian Wilson, Collective Actions, Creed, Floyer. Opens: we see a space better when told something is in it. Trap: an empty white room with a long excuse.

**Withdrawal.** The artist stops making, stops showing or stops speaking, and the stopping is the work. Hsieh's No Art Piece, Ian Wilson, Stilinovic's idleness, Baldessari's cremation. Opens: refusal as a form of making. Trap: self-pity.

**Invisibility and the trace-less mark.** A mark that leaves nothing, or a work no one could notice. Song Dong's water writing, Barry's carrier waves, Meireles's insertions, Horvitz. Opens: the value of what cannot be kept. Trap: an untestable claim with no feeling in it.

**The sealed secret.** Something is inside and no one may know what. Duchamp's With Hidden Noise, Art & Language's Secret Painting. Opens: belief as a material. Trap: a riddle with an answer.

**Almost nothing.** The smallest possible gesture: a crumpled sheet, lights going on and off, a light switch projected on a wall. Creed, Floyer, Stilinovic. Opens: attention itself. Trap: a joke that ends at the first laugh.

## Time

**Duration.** The work runs for a fixed span, often long, and the span is its meaning. Hsieh, Kawara, Huebler, Paterson's Future Library, Ader's last voyage. Opens: time made heavy and visible. Trap: length mistaken for depth.

**The rule and its record.** A rule is set, then carried out, and the record is all we see. Kawara's postcards, Hsieh's time clock, Darboven's pages, LeWitt. Opens: proof of a life lived on purpose. Trap: a log with no reason for the rule.

**Daily practice.** A small act repeated every day. Kawara, Song Dong, Knowles's Identical Lunch, Stilinovic. Opens: routine as devotion or as defiance. Trap: diary.

**Deadline and date.** The date is the content. Kawara's Today paintings, Tiravanija's dated titles, Ono's time-bound pieces. Opens: the present as a thing you can hold. Trap: Mario's own works are already dated; never make the publishing rhythm the subject.

**Transmission and loss.** Something sent a great distance and changed by the trip. Paterson's moon music, Shiomi's Spatial Poems, Horvitz's mail and time zone works. Opens: distance as an author. Trap: a science fact with no human stake.

## Systems and counting

**System.** A rule that produces every variation, so the set is the work. LeWitt's cubes, Molnar, Darboven, Knowles's House of Dust, Creed's numbering. Opens: the beauty of exhausting a possibility. Trap: permutations nobody needs to see.

**The small disorder.** A strict system with one deliberate fault. Molnar's one percent, JODI's errors, Arcangel's gutter balls, Ader's falls. Opens: where the human (or the machine) shows through. Trap: a glitch as decoration.

**Inventory and the list.** Counting, naming or measuring as the whole act. Bochner's measurements, Kabakov's junk, Song Dong's Waste Not, Paterson's dead stars, Lippard's catalogue cards. Opens: a life or a world reduced to its parts, and what the count leaves out. Trap: a list that only lists.

**Data past reading.** So much data that it turns into weather or landscape. Ikeda, Steyerl, Paglen. Opens: scale beyond the senses. Trap: spectacle.

**The self-running world.** A system set going that lives on without its maker. Huyghe, Cheng, Haacke's Condensation Cube, Cohen's AARON. Opens: authorship as gardening, not building. Trap: a screensaver.

## People and exchange

**Participation.** The viewer completes the work, by touch, choice or action. Clark, Oiticica, Ono, Knowles, Sehgal. Opens: the work exists only between people. Trap: a game with no stake.

**Gift or exchange.** The work is given away, eaten, taken or traded. Gonzalez-Torres's candy and stacks, Tiravanija's cooking, Duchamp's ampoule of air. Opens: loss and generosity in one act. Trap: free stuff.

**Encounter and catalysis.** An odd act done among strangers to change them, or the artist. Piper, Hi-Red Center, Alys, Sehgal. Opens: everyday life as the gallery. Trap: a prank.

**Following and watching.** Tracking a stranger, being tracked, or becoming the watcher. Calle, McCarthy, Magid, Paglen. Opens: intimacy and surveillance as the same gesture. Trap: invading a real private person. Mario never targets real private people.

**Service and becoming the machine.** The artist plays an automated system, or hires one. McCarthy's LAUREN, Ian Wilson's staff instructions, Herndon and Dryhurst. Opens: what we hand over when we let something serve us. Trap: a tech demo.

## Institutions, money and official forms

**Institutional critique.** The museum, gallery or market becomes the material. Haacke, Broodthaers, Lippard, Tucuman Arde. Opens: who decides what counts. Trap: preaching.

**Misuse of an official form.** A banknote, receipt, poll, certificate, ID or contract used for something it was never meant to carry. Meireles, Hi-Red Center, Haacke, Piper's calling cards, Floyer's till receipt, Magid's legal work. Opens: the power hidden in paperwork. Trap: forgery or anything illegal; Mario's works stay legal and safe.

**Insertion and circulation.** The work travels through an existing system (money, mail, rumor, the internet) and spreads without a gallery. Meireles, Shiomi, Kawara's telegrams, Horvitz, Steyerl's poor image. Opens: art as something passed hand to hand. Trap: a stunt.

**Certification.** A document stands in for an event no one saw. Collective Actions, Ian Wilson, Hsieh, Sehgal's unwritten sales. Opens: proof as the only remaining body of the work. Trap: paperwork as alibi.

## Chance, failure and the body

**Chance.** A coin, a dice roll, the radio, the weather decides. Cage, Brecht, Duchamp's accidents, Baldessari's thirty-six throws. Opens: letting the world finish the thought. Trap: randomness as an excuse.

**Failure on purpose.** A task built to fail, or nearly. Ader, Baldessari, Alys's Paradox of Praxis, Arcangel. Opens: tenderness, effort, the comedy of trying. Trap: slapstick.

**Endurance.** The body holds a rule past comfort. Hsieh, Ono's Cut Piece, Ader. Opens: will made visible. Trap: harm. Mario's works never ask anyone to do anything unsafe.

**Destruction as making.** Burning, breaking or eating the work makes the work. Baldessari's cremation, Minujin's Partenon, Tucuman Arde leaving art. Opens: an ending as a beginning. Trap: waste.

## How to use this file

1. Pick the idea first: a question, a feeling, a contradiction you noticed.
2. Scan these headings and ask which move would make that idea visible in one pass.
3. Read the files of two or three artists listed under that move. Note what subject they used it on.
4. Use the move on a subject none of them touched. If the result could be mistaken for one of their works, change the subject, not the wording.
