# Ilya Kabakov

Soviet-born, 1933 (Dnipropetrovsk, now Dnipro, Ukraine) to 2023. Unofficial artist in Moscow until 1987, then based in the West. After his move west, works are credited jointly with Emilia Kabakov (born 1945).

## Who
Kabakov was a leading figure of Moscow Conceptualism. He earned his living as an official children's book illustrator (well known, not confirmed on a page read here) while making unofficial work for a small circle, and his attic studio on Sretensky Boulevard was a meeting point for that scene. He invented fictional characters and gave them albums, rooms and voices, then built whole rooms he called "total installations." After emigrating in 1987 he and Emilia made more than 150 installations, shown at the Venice Biennale, the Hermitage, Tate Modern and elsewhere.

## Key works
- Ten Characters (albums), early 1970s. A series of picture-and-text albums, each about a lonely fictional man, such as Sitting in a Closet Primakov and The Flying Komarov. He read them aloud to friends in his studio, page by page. [checked]
- The Answers of an Experimental Group, 1970 to 1971. An object-painting that mocks Soviet collective life and its utopian claims. [checked]
- The Man Who Flew into Space from His Apartment, 1985, first made in his Moscow studio (Centre Pompidou). A shabby room with posters on the walls, a catapult seat hanging from a hole in the ceiling and rubble on the floor. Viewers look in through boards. The man has escaped and left only traces. [checked]
- Ten Characters (installation), Ronald Feldman Gallery, New York, 1988. A communal apartment of separate rooms, each the home of a character, including the space man. [checked]
- The Man Who Never Threw Anything Away (The Garbage Man), 1988 to 1995. The room of a man in a communal flat filled with sorted junk he cannot discard. [checked]
- Labyrinth (My Mother's Album), 1990. A narrow corridor maze lined with his mother's memoir and photographs. [checked]
- Not Everyone Will Be Taken into the Future, 2001 (with Emilia Kabakov). A whole-room work whose title comes from a 1983 text in which he imagined Malevich picking students for summer camp. [checked]

## Core moves
- Fictional author: invent a character and let the work be theirs.
- Total installation: build a complete room so the visitor enters a story, not a picture.
- Trace of an absent person: show the room after the event, never the event.
- The album: a private book performed aloud to a small audience.
- Inventory of junk: everyday rubbish catalogued as if precious.
- Utopia from below: grand dreams (flight, the future) staged in poor, cramped places.

## Ideas worth keeping
Kabakov shows how an ordinary person survives an official ideology by dreaming. His characters are small, tired, often absurd, but each has a private project: flying into space, keeping every scrap, hiding in a closet. The state promised the cosmos; his man really goes, from a communal flat, with a homemade catapult. The joke and the sadness are the same thing.

He also treats authorship as fiction. By giving the work to invented people, he escapes the demand to speak as a heroic artist and can tell many voices at once. The empty room becomes a stage on which the viewer imagines what happened. Tate's 2017 press release reads the title Not Everyone Will Be Taken into the Future as an allegory of which artists history keeps and which it leaves behind.

## What Mario could learn
- Invent an unremarkable person with one private obsession, and make the work their evidence.
- Show the room after the act, so the viewer reconstructs the story.
- Set a huge dream inside a small, poor space and let the contrast carry the meaning.
- Use a list or archive of worthless things to describe a whole life.
- Do not reuse: a man escaping into space from his flat, a catapult through a ceiling, a man who keeps all his garbage, communal-flat rooms for ten characters, Malevich choosing students for summer camp.
