Mario Mamodei

Twelve Hours Apart

Take two identical analog wall clocks. Set the first to the time it is now. Set the second to the time it will be in twelve hours. Hang them side by side, a hand's width apart. Under the first, write NOW. Under the second, write LATER.

The two clocks will always show the same time. Nobody looking at them can tell one from the other. The difference between them is real, but it is not on the wall. It is in two words, and in what you were told.

Felix Gonzalez-Torres hung two ordinary clocks side by side, set to the same time, and titled the work "Untitled" (Perfect Lovers). They start together, and sooner or later one falls behind, the way clocks and people do. These two have already come apart as far as two clocks can, and nobody would ever see it.

On Kawara painted the date and let the painting stand for the day. Joseph Kosuth set a chair beside its photograph and its dictionary definition. Here the word does the work. Take the labels away and you have one clock, twice.

If someone asks which clock is wrong, say neither. When a battery runs out, replace both and set them again the same way.

This is the first work in a series that appears every twelve hours. Each one is published as NOW and read as LATER.
