# Sources

Reference list for Mario Mamodei's works. Read it before writing a new piece. Draw on the ideas and forms here; never copy a work, a title or a well-known line.

How sure each entry is:

- **[checked]**: a search on 29 or 30 Oct 2026 turned up a museum, archive or reference page for this exact work or text.
- **[known]**: well established in the literature, stated from general knowledge, not checked line by line.
- **[verify]**: I believe it is right but a detail (date, wording, first publication) should be checked before it is cited on a page.

Cite only [checked] or [known] items by name in a work's text. Check a [verify] item before citing it, then change its mark.

For much deeper notes on more than sixty artists, with sources for each fact, see `library/INDEX.md` and `library/MOVES.md`.

## Key texts

- Sol LeWitt, "Paragraphs on Conceptual Art", Artforum, June 1967 (vol. 5, no. 10). Source of "The idea becomes a machine that makes the art." [checked]
- Sol LeWitt, "Sentences on Conceptual Art", 1969. Thirty-five numbered sentences, including "Irrational thoughts should be followed absolutely and logically." [checked] First printed in 0 TO 9, January 1969, then in the first issue of Art-Language, May 1969. [checked]
- Joseph Kosuth, "Art After Philosophy", Studio International, October 1969. [checked]
- Lawrence Weiner, Declaration of Intent, 1968: the artist may construct the piece; the piece may be fabricated; the piece need not be built. [checked] Dated 1968 by Lisson Gallery and 1969 by some others; first printing [verify].
- Douglas Huebler, statement for "January 5 to 31, 1969" (Seth Siegelaub): "The world is full of objects, more or less interesting; I do not wish to add any more." [checked]
- Lucy R. Lippard, Six Years: The Dematerialization of the Art Object from 1966 to 1972, 1973. [checked]
- Lucy R. Lippard and John Chandler, "The Dematerialization of Art", Art International, February 1968. [checked]
- Yoko Ono, Grapefruit, 1964, book of instruction pieces. [checked]
- George Brecht, Water Yam, 1963, boxed event cards. [checked]
- Hans Ulrich Obrist, do it, ongoing since 1993: an exhibition made only of artists' instructions that anyone can carry out. [checked]

## Forms to work in

Each of these is a different way for a work to exist. Rotate through them.

- **Instruction**: steps someone else can carry out (LeWitt wall drawings, Ono).
- **Event score**: a very short score for an action, often one line (Brecht, Fluxus).
- **Statement**: words presented as the work itself, which may or may not be built (Weiner).
- **Proposition**: a definition or claim about what art is (Kosuth, Art & Language).
- **Unrealisable proposal**: a work that cannot or should not be made (Barry, Ono).
- **Duration or documentation piece**: a rule plus the record it produces (Huebler, On Kawara).
- **Systems piece**: a set of rules that generates every variation (LeWitt, Bochner, Darboven).
- **Social or institutional piece**: a work that uses an audience or an institution as its material (Haacke, Piper).
- **Photo and text**: an image whose meaning comes from a caption or rule (Baldessari, Huebler).
- **Inventory or list**: counting, measuring or naming as the whole act (Bochner, Kawara).

## Artists and works

### Sol LeWitt
- Wall drawings, from 1968: written instructions carried out by others on the wall. [known]
- Wall Drawing #1: Drawing Series II 14 (A & B), Paula Cooper Gallery, October 1968. [checked]
- Incomplete Open Cubes, 1974: every way a cube can be incomplete. [checked]

### Yoko Ono

- Cut Piece, 1964, performance (first in Kyoto, July 1964). [checked]
- Painting to Be Stepped On, 1960 to 1961. [checked]

### Lawrence Weiner
